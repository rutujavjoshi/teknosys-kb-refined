# Structuring for Readability

Even good content fails if users can’t scan it quickly.

Most users don’t read documentation line by line—they scan for what they need.

Structure makes that possible.

---

## Why this matters

- Improves findability  
- Reduces cognitive load  
- Helps users complete tasks faster  
- Makes content easier to maintain  

---

## How users read

Users typically:

- Scan headings  
- Look for keywords  
- Jump between sections  

They rarely read top to bottom.

---

## The structure model

Effective content follows this pattern:


Title
↓
Headings
↓
Chunks
↓
Lists / Steps


---

## Key techniques

### 1. Chunking

Break content into small, manageable sections.

❌ Long paragraphs  
✔ Short sections with clear headings  

---

### 2. Headings

Use headings to guide navigation.

- Keep them descriptive  
- Avoid vague titles like “Overview”  

---

### 3. Lists and steps

Use:
- Bullet points → for information  
- Numbered steps → for tasks  

---

### 4. Visual hierarchy

Make important information stand out:
- Headings  
- Bold text (limited use)  
- Spacing  

---

## Common mistakes

- Dense paragraphs  
- No clear section breaks  
- Overuse of bold text  
- Inconsistent structure  

---

## Practical guidance

- One idea per section  
- Keep paragraphs under 4–5 lines  
- Use headings every 200–300 words  
- Think: *Can this be scanned in 10 seconds?*
