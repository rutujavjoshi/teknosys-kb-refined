# Style & Consistency

Good documentation is not just correct—it is consistent.

Consistency reduces effort for the user. They don’t have to “re-learn” how to read your content.

---

## Why this matters

- Improves readability  
- Builds trust  
- Reduces confusion  
- Makes large documentation sets manageable  

---

## What consistency means

Consistency applies to:

- Tone and voice  
- Terminology  
- Formatting  
- Structure  

---

## Tone and voice

Your tone should be:

- Clear  
- Direct  
- Neutral  
- Helpful  

Avoid:
- Overly formal language  
- Conversational fluff  
- Developer-heavy jargon (unless required)

---

## Terminology

Use the same word for the same thing.

❌ “Click Submit” / “Press Submit” / “Select Submit”  
✔ Pick one and use it consistently  

---

## Formatting

Be consistent with:

- Headings  
- Lists  
- Code blocks  
- Notes  

Users should recognize patterns across pages.

---

## Style guides

A style guide helps standardize:

- Grammar rules  
- Terminology  
- Formatting patterns  

Even a simple internal guide makes a big difference.

---

## Common mistakes

- Switching tone across pages  
- Inconsistent terminology  
- Mixing styles (formal vs casual)  
- Over-formatting content  

---

## Practical guidance

- Create a lightweight style guide early  
- Review content for consistency, not just correctness  
- Reuse patterns across pages  
- Keep formatting predictable
